# Stone and specialist finishes

Start with the adopted standard's finish and decoration rules, plus relevant waterproofing, substrates, joinery and fire-protection operations. Select from the actual operation rather than the material name alone; read complete source tables before applying units, deductions or inclusions.

Inspect finish schedules, plans, elevations, sections and fabrication details. Establish material/grade, thickness, panel/tile size, pattern, grain/bookmatching, joint layout, edge profiles, curvature, backing/base, bedding/fixing, waterproofing, sealing/polishing and tolerance requirements. Separate existing preparation/repairs from new work where scope differs. Identify stairs, risers, skirtings, returns, reveals and narrow strips without assuming they share the main area's unit or rate.

Read the source table for the affected finish and its deductions, descriptions and inclusions. Record the net measured quantity, fabrication/procurement quantity and any supported waste allowance separately. Pattern matching, breakage, spare stock and slab yield need project/supplier evidence; never impose a standard luxury-material waste percentage. Waste is not automatically added to installation labour.

Reconcile owner material purchases with the installer's quotation. Check who owns freight, customs, storage, handling, cutting, fixing materials, substrates, ordinary bedding, additional levelling, protection and final cleaning. Retain supplied quotes and their rate basis. Do not count ordinary bedding inside an installation rate and again as floor levelling; use the user's supplied company definition if coding is required.

Price mock-ups, samples, specialist access, sequencing and protection only where supported scope or an explicit scenario requires them. Maintain queries for unspecified finish grades, substrate condition, fabrication boundaries or missing local prices. Preserve brand/function and maintenance consequences in value-engineering comparisons.
