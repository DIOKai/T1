# Commercial control and practical advice

## Tender comparison

Retain original submissions and qualifications. Establish each price boundary, currency, tax, date, validity, exclusions, programme and commercial terms. Compare to a common scope and show original bid + evidenced additions − evidenced deductions = comparable amount. Keep contractor-confirmed adjustments separate from client scenario allowances and unpriced queries. Do not treat a supplied omission allowance as a revised contractor offer.

Normalise genuinely different inclusions, quantities, taxes, currencies and pricing dates with explicit evidence. Unknown omissions should remain an exposure or scenario, not an invented binding price. Check alternates, provisional sums, design responsibility, risk allocation, warranties, performance, long leads and deliverability. Identify material commercial exceptions, conditional rankings and clarifications; a cheapest noncompliant bid is not the recommendation. A review does not authorise award.

## Budget, commitments, actuals and forecast

Define cut-off, row grain, identifiers and states before joining data. Keep original approved budget, current approved budget, latest forecast, current commitment, paid cash, accrued cost, uncommitted remaining work, pending changes and risk distinguishable. Tax/FX and gross/net bases must match. Use a reconciliation rather than adding different ledgers indiscriminately.

Two useful forecast constructions, chosen by actual data semantics:

- incurred cost + remaining cost to complete + exposures not already inside either component;
- current commitments including their paid portion + uncommitted work + net forecast changes/risk outside those commitments.

Do not add actual payments again to a full commitment. An invoice may represent an accrual rather than cash paid. A revised total PO replaces its predecessor; a delta amendment adds only if not already included. Link base PO, revisions, change ID and inclusion point; deduplicate before totals. If semantics are unknown, show the alternatives and the query needed to decide. Remaining cost may already include pending changes or risk; test that boundary first.

Calculate budget variance with a stated sign (e.g. forecast minus approved budget = overrun). A budget transfer reallocates approval and should net to zero; it does not change forecast scope. A recode changes classification without changing total. Do not report uncommitted budget or a delay in invoicing as savings. Reconcile prior to new forecast with explanatory movements and retain unresolved differences.

Change logs need description/source, quantity/rate basis, delta versus revised-total treatment, instruction/approval status, responsibility, commitment inclusion and forecast inclusion. Approval status and likelihood of cost occurrence are different. Forecast realistic exposure even while approval is pending, labelled appropriately; never claim legal entitlement from cost arithmetic. Read actual clauses/notices/valuation basis and current authoritative local requirements when entitlement matters.

## Cash flow

Identify perspective: owner payment forecast, contractor receipts or work value. Use programme and payment terms: deposits, progress/milestones, payment lag, retention cap/release, tax and advance recovery. Do not silently insert a generic S-curve. Separate earned/certified value from due payment and cash timing. Never count a recoverable advance twice as additional contract cost.

For each period show gross earned/certified amount, advance/recovery, retention movement, net amount due, payment period and cumulative cash. Extend the horizon for lag and retention releases. Reconcile total gross value, retained balance, advances outstanding and cash; disclose terminal unpaid liabilities if the horizon ends early. Negative cash movements and release dates require contract/input support. Taxability and recovery timing need the actual local basis.

## Risk and contingency

Distinguish design incompleteness, named risks, provisional sums, management reserve and inflation. Each risk needs scope, cause, effect, owner, evidence and inclusion point. Expected value p × impact may support an allowance; it does not describe a tail percentile. Scenarios or distributions must state assumptions, dependencies and correlation. Do not label a simple range P80. Check that an included risk allowance is not added again as a pending change. State contingency drawdown policy only if supplied; approval rules are not universal.

## Value engineering and process advice

Compare baseline and option at equivalent installed scope: purchase, installation, redesign, testing, logistics, programme and consequential works. Net savings exclude nothing material; identify provisional consequences. Test mandatory requirements first. Then consider brand/design, durability, warranty, maintenance, operating cost and residual/disposal effects. Cost reductions dependent on approvals need named dependencies and decision owners; do not assert design equivalence without the relevant evidence. Use [lifecycle.md](lifecycle.md) for whole-life comparisons.

Diagnose process from a representative record, not a presumed enterprise workflow. Distinguish observed practice, stated policy and proposed practice. Propose the simplest remedy for the demonstrated failure with an owner, adoption effort and success measure. A small refresh may need a versioned schedule and query list. A complex building may justify interface reviews and a change/commitment reconciliation. Fix a missing budget/forecast distinction before demanding unverified finance-system integration. Recommendations should make a current decision easier and remain proportionate to consequence and uncertainty.
