# Maintenance, renewals and lifecycle economics

Define the question before choosing the method: annual maintenance budget, renewal events, nominal cash flow, discounted life-cycle cost or broader whole-life comparison. Cost divided by life is not a universal substitute for any of these. State asset population, condition/age, service lives, tasks/frequencies, quantities, labour/productivity, material rates, access and management cost boundaries; standards and worked examples do not establish these project inputs.

Keep capital construction, operation, maintenance, renewal and disposal/residual value distinct. Agreed analysis scope determines whether revenues, externalities and wider costs enter a whole-life model. Cost and carbon are separate measures requiring their own data and units; a lower price does not prove lower carbon.

For each asset/event retain asset ID, location, material/specification, quantity/unit, task, interval, source, expected life and remaining-life assumption. A reference service life is not a surveyed remaining life. Frequency conversions must distinguish every n years from n times/year. State whether an access input is a multiplier or a percentage increase; 1.25 and +25% are equivalent here, 25 and 0.25 are not.

Where NRM 3 classification is adopted, use the requested table/version and check separate maintenance versus renewal units. The 2026 table update is separate from the 2021 core volume; do not silently join lowest-level codes across editions. Classification does not supply current statutory maintenance obligations or task prices. Use lawful supplied maintenance guidance and verify applicability.

For discounted comparisons:

- State base date, study period, currency, real/nominal convention, discount rate, escalation, event timing, end boundary and residual/disposal treatment.
- End-of-year present value is PV = sum(C_t / (1+r)^t), with initial costs at t=0; use another timing convention only when specified.
- For constant real costs, discount at the real rate. For nominal costs, escalate to event-year cash and discount at the nominal rate. When consistent common inflation is intended, (1+r_nominal) = (1+r_real)(1+inflation); do not subtract rates as an exact identity.
- List renewal years from remaining life and repeat interval. Explicitly include/exclude a replacement at the study-period end; avoid buying an asset with no service benefit under an unstated boundary convention. Avoid counting a year-zero replacement both as capital and renewal.
- Treat residual value as a signed credit at the stated year. Different study lives need a declared common comparison basis; no silent annualisation.

Show event schedules and subtotals; reconcile capital + maintenance + renewal + other costs − credits to PV. Check discount factors, event counts, years, rates and units with calculation tools. Test sensitivity where a life or rate assumption could change the decision. The lowest PV option is eligible only if required performance/maintenance and programme constraints are satisfied. Cite actual source clauses when claiming a specific standard calculation; a generic PV model is not proof of formal standard compliance.
