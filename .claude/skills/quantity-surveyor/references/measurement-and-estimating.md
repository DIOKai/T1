# Measurement and estimating

## Measurement and bills

Choose area/functional-unit benchmarking for adequately defined early scope, elemental planning for developing design, and detailed take-off for supported packages. Do not interchange gross, net, usable or rentable area denominators. Benchmarks need comparable typology, scope, area convention, date, location, procurement and inclusions. A budget target is not evidence of a market rate.

Read the adopted work-section rule with its units, descriptions, deductions, inclusions, exclusions and notes. Do not apply a finish deduction threshold to structural work or infer a rule from a search snippet. User-specified arithmetic conventions can support a controlled take-off without claiming formal compliance. Keep bill measurement separate from physical procurement quantities and waste.

Record item ID, drawing/specification revision, location, description, dimension string and multiplicity, calculation, unit and rule/source locator. Convert dimensions before multiplication (mm to m; mm² to m²; mm³ to m³). Include thickness when converting area to volume. Identify scale and calibration on each relevant sheet; printed scale can fail after resizing. Do not guess inaccessible dimensions from photographs. If visual inspection is unavailable, produce only the supported dimension-based schedule and state what drawing review remains.

Separate rooms, floors, elevations, phases and buildings; reconcile repeated modules to counts and avoid common works appearing on every building. Distinguish openings, reveals, returns, soffits, interfaces, junctions and extra operations as the selected rule requires. Clarify conflicts between stated dimensions, geometry and revisions. Do not silently resolve design conflicts. Net measurement, procurement rounding and billed quantity may differ; show each basis.

## Building work and package interfaces

Use these as completeness prompts tied to actual scope, not automatic priced additions:

| Scope | Interfaces to inspect |
|---|---|
| Building fit-out and alterations | demolition/strip-out; landlord/base-build tie-ins; partitions and finishes; stone/timber/metal detailing; bespoke millwork; display fixtures; lighting; services alterations; security, IT and AV; signage; specialist rooms; accessibility and life safety |
| Direct purchase | who specifies, supplies, ships, clears customs, stores, installs, insures, protects and warrants each fixture/material; spare stock; samples; installer coordination |
| Building envelope | framing and cladding/glazing; backing and supports; anchors and structural openings; waterproofing; drainage and movement joints; thermal/fire/acoustic performance; scaffold/cranes/access; mock-ups, shop drawings, testing and approval interfaces |
| Programme | restricted/night work, logistics/storage, temporary protection/security, continued occupancy, site/landlord constraints, long-lead procurement and handover commissioning |

An all-in building-envelope quote may include supports/testing; examine its inclusions before adding separate allowances. Removing owner supply from a contractor quote must preserve installation and ancillary responsibility. Design performance and permission costs need project-specific evidence; appearance alone does not specify material quality.

## Whole-project scope

Build a coverage matrix: scope, responsible package, evidence, pricing status, inclusion point, unresolved interface. Start from the whole brief, site boundary and programme, not only the individual work package. Inspect, as relevant: surveys/ground investigation; demolition and hazardous materials; enabling works; groundworks, earthworks, dewatering and temporary support; foundations and frame; upper floors, stairs and roof; external envelope; interiors; mechanical, electrical, plumbing and specialist systems; lifts; utilities/diversions/connections; roads, drainage, landscaping and external works; testing/commissioning/handover; contractor preliminaries; design/consultant/permit costs and owner direct costs. Land, finance, taxes and operational costs belong only where the requested cost boundary includes them; disclose exclusions explicitly.

Separate each building from shared site/infrastructure and allocate common costs once with a stated basis. Interfaces include ground conditions, utility capacity, fire strategy, structural/MEP coordination, temporary works, weather protection, site access and commissioning. A partial set of package prices supports a subtotal, not a complete construction budget. Do not fabricate unit costs to conceal missing packages.

In a whole-project completeness review, explicitly check external lighting, contractor overhead/profit and accessibility/authority review when relevant. Show their package inclusion or unpriced status rather than assuming they sit inside a broad services or preliminaries label. Confirm addition bases before pricing.

## Rates and cost plans

Record quantity, unit, rate, extension, evidence/source, geography, currency, price date and inclusions. A crew build-up can use:

- crew labour cost/hour = sum of people × wage/hour × stated burden factor;
- production cost/unit = (crew labour + equipment cost/hour) / crew output/unit per hour;
- material/unit = net requirement × stated waste factor × price, plus evidenced freight/duty/consumables;
- direct unit cost = labour + materials + equipment + other measured costs;
- tender rate = direct unit cost plus additions on their explicitly stated bases.

Crew output differs from per-person productivity. State shift length, efficiency or restricted-hours assumptions only where provided/supported. Material waste does not automatically inflate installation labour. Burden, overhead and profit percentages differ from margin on sale (price = cost/(1−margin)). Do not apply both unknowingly. Hour/day, linear/area/volume and pack/unit prices need explicit conversion. Retain discounts/credits with signed values and defined bases.

Apply indices as target/base when they represent the selected cost basket and period; do not combine an already rebased quote with the same escalation again. State FX as target currency per source currency (or its inverse explicitly); distinguish exchange assumptions from local inflation. Expose fees, preliminaries, owner costs, risk, escalation and tax, and confirm what rates already cover. Identify estimate-stage uncertainty from actual gaps rather than an automatic stage percentage. Reconcile revisions by quantities, rates, scope, programme, FX, inflation and risk without hiding a residual.

## Coding and schedules

Use the user's supplied code definitions and approved version. Separate measurement classification, procurement packages and financial codes; they need not have a one-to-one relationship. Split mixed scope when evidence permits. Confirm mapping only when definitions and inclusions support it; otherwise label candidates provisional or retain unallocated amounts with queries. A provisional code is not a provisional price, and an approved price is not approved coding. Preserve credits and total value across any crosswalk; never force an unresolved amount into the nearest label.

Use [estimate.csv](../templates/estimate.csv) as an optional starting schema. Retain detailed take-off lines behind aggregated items. Check extensions, percentages, unit scale, repeated scope, package coverage and all totals before delivery. Distinguish checked calculations from untouched workbook cached values.
